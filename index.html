Research focus

This work explores how changing the ionomer-to-carbon (I/C) ratio from 0.6 to 0.4 alters proton conductivity, gas diffusion, and water management within a membrane electrode assembly (MEA) in a single fuel cell.

The electrochemical performance of the fabricated MEAs was investigated using in-situ steady-state polarization profiling and Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) on a Hephas testing station.

Hypothesis

Reducing the I/C ratio from 0.6 to 0.4 will thin out the ionomer film and optimize the pore network, reducing oxygen transport resistance and enhancing water removal in the mass-transport-limited, high-current-density regions of the fuel cell.

Fabrication & assembly

Catalyst Layer Fabrication

Dispersed platinum supported on carbon (Pt/C) nanoparticles in an isopropanol/DI water solvent matrix with liquid Nafion ionomer. Ultrasonic bath sonication and mechanical stirring disintegrated agglomerates before deposition. The I/C mass ratio was adjusted from 0.6 to 0.4 to balance continuous proton-conducting pathways and open pore networks for reactant gas transport.

Membrane Electrode Assembly (MEA)

Deposited anode and cathode catalyst inks onto opposing faces of a PFSA/Nafion membrane to fabricate the catalyst-coated membrane (CCM). The NR211 PEM was 25 μm thick. Controlled hot pressing consolidated the catalyst layer–membrane interface. Hydrophobic carbon-paper Gas Diffusion Layers (GDLs), Microporous Layers (MPLs), and PTFE sub-gaskets completed the assembly.

Single Cell PEMFC Assembly

Aligned the MEA between graphite bipolar plates with serpentine flow channels, gold-plated copper current collectors, and aluminum end plates. Perimeter tie-bolts were torqued incrementally in a crisscross pattern to achieve uniform compression, minimize interfacial contact resistance, and preserve GDL pore structure.

Electrochemical characterization

The assembled cell was integrated into a fuel cell test station with regulated temperature, mass flow, and humidification.

Polarization Curve

Steady-state I–V sweeps characterize cell voltage under load. The polarization framework separates activation, ohmic, and concentration overpotentials.

Vcell = ENernst − ηact − ηohm − ηconc

Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy

EIS Nyquist plots were used to investigate the contributions of activation, ohmic, and mass transport losses.

Fundamentals, reactant pathways, MEA structure, polarization curves, literature review, and experimentation.
